## Gmail API & Google OAuth policy

- **Gmail scope classification (current official table): NON-SENSITIVE: gmail.labels, gmail.addons.current.action.compose, gmail.addons.current.message.action. SENSITIVE: gmail.send, gmail.addons.current.message.metadata, gmail.addons.current.message.readonly. RESTRICTED: https://mail.google.com/, gmail.readonly, gmail.metadata, gmail.modify, gmail.compose, gmail.insert, gmail.settings.basic, gmail.settings.sharing.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes> · <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464325?hl=en> · <https://developers.google.com/workspace/workspace-api-user-data-developer-policy>
- **openid / email / profile (userinfo.email, userinfo.profile, openid) are the basic Sign-In scopes. They count as non-sensitive and are exempt from the Testing-mode 7-day expiry and warning screen.** _(H, live)_ — <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945?hl=en> · <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/sensitive-scope-verification>
- **gmail.metadata is RESTRICTED, just like gmail.readonly. It returns only IDs, labels and headers (no body). It cannot use the q= search parameter, and it cannot use format=full or format=raw.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.messages/list> · <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/Format> · <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes>
- **gmail.labels (non-sensitive) only manages label objects. Putting labels on messages (messages.modify) needs mail.google.com or gmail.modify, which are restricted. There is also a gmail.modify.restricted scope that is not in the public scope table.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users.messages/modify> · <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/labels>
- **CASA security assessment trigger: 'If you store restricted scope data on servers (or transmit), then you must go through a security assessment.' The trigger also covers any app that 'has the ability to access data from or through a third-party server'. It repeats at least every 12 months.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification> · <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/auth/scopes> · <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463816?hl=en>
- **Verification timelines (Google's estimates, 'not guaranteed'): Brand verification 2-3 business days. Sensitive scope verification 10 business days (FAQ), though the sensitive-scope page says 'typically takes 3-5 business days'. Restricted scope verification about 6 weeks including the security assessment.** _(H, live)_ — <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en> · <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/restricted-scope-verification> · <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/production-readiness/sensitive-scope-verification>

- **CASA now uses assurance levels AL1 and AL2 (legacy Tiers 2 and 3). Google, not the developer, decides the level from data sensitivity, user count and risk signals. Both levels are done by ADA-authorized labs. Google charges nothing, and the lab bills the developer directly.** _(M, live)_ — <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13465431?hl=en> · <https://appdefensealliance.dev/casa/casa-tiering> · <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en>

Any non-basic scope makes authorizations and refresh tokens expire 7 days after consent.** _(H, live)_ — <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945?hl=en> · <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2>

Once the cap is used up, Google sign-in can be disabled.** _(H, live)_ — <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/15549945?hl=en> · <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13463817?hl=en> · <https://support.google.com/cloud/answer/13464323?hl=en>

Watch must be renewed at least every 7 days, and Google recommends once per day.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/push> · <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/reference/rest/v1/users/watch>

Notifications can be delayed or dropped, so you need a periodic history.list fallback.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/workspace/gmail/api/guides/push> · <https://cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/push> · <https://cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/subscription-overview>
- **Authenticated push: Pub/Sub sends 'Authorization: Bearer <OIDC JWT>' signed by Google. Verify the signature, check that aud equals the configured audience (if none is set, 'the Push endpoint URL will be used'), check that email equals your push-auth service account and email_verified is true, and check that iss is accounts.google.com or https://accounts.google.com.** _(H, live)_ — <https://cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/authenticate-push-subscriptions> · <https://cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/reference/rest/v1/projects.subscriptions> · <https://cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/create-push-subscription>
- **Pub/Sub subscription defaults that matter here: ackDeadlineSeconds defaults to 10 (range 10-600), and for push it is also the HTTP request timeout. expirationPolicy defaults to a 31-day TTL of inactivity, after which the subscription is deleted automatically. messageRetentionDuration defaults to 7 days.** _(H, live)_ — <https://cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/reference/rest/v1/projects.subscriptions> · <https://cloud.google.com/pubsub/docs/push>
- **Getting tokens: access_type=offline returns a refresh token only on the FIRST code exchange. prompt=consent forces re-consent so a new refresh token is issued. include_granted_scopes=true turns on incremental authorization. Users can grant only some of the requested scopes (granular consent), so the app must check which scopes were granted.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/web-server> · <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2>

The limit is 100 refresh tokens per Google Account per OAuth client ID, and when it's reached the oldest is invalidated silently.** _(H, live)_ — <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2> · <https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/web-server>

That is headers List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click plus List-Unsubscribe: <https://...> (RFC 8058 and RFC 2369). The spam-rate threshold is 0.30%.** _(H, live)_ — <https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126?hl=en>
- **Microsoft Graph phase 2: delegated Mail.ReadBasic (all properties except body, previewBody, attachments and extended properties) and Mail.Read both work with personal Microsoft accounts. Use delta per mailFolder. Message subscriptions last at most 10,080 minutes (under 7 days), or 1,440 minutes when resource data is included.** _(H, live)_ — <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/permissions-reference> · <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/message-delta?view=graph-rest-1.0> · <https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/subscription?view=graph-rest-1.0>

**Gotchas**

- NEW 2026 QUOTAS: a Cloud project created on or after 2026-05-01 gets 6,000 units per user per minute (was 15,000), and messages.get now costs 20 units (was 5). That is about 5 messages.get per second per user, a 10x drop. A naive 'fetch all 30k emails on connect' backfill will take hours per user and hit 429/403 errors.
- The quota cost is per method, not per format. format=metadata saves bandwidth but costs the same 20 units as format=full, so fetch each message once in full when you'll need the body anyway.
- gmail.metadata is also RESTRICTED. It gives no verification or CASA savings over gmail.readonly, it forbids q= search, and it forbids format=full/raw. Choose gmail.readonly.
- Any server-side processing of Gmail data (Vercel functions, workers, DB, sending to Claude) triggers the annual CASA assessment. You can't launch publicly to more than 100 users without restricted-scope verification plus a Letter of Validation, which takes about 6 weeks or more. Plan the launch around this.
- The 100-user unverified cap covers the project's ENTIRE lifetime and can't be reset. Don't burn it on a production project with casual testers. Use a separate dev/test project (Testing status with listed test users) and a separate production project.
- In Testing mode with any Gmail scope, refresh tokens die 7 days after consent. Background sync will break weekly for testers unless they reconnect. This is expected, not a bug.
- Google returns a refresh token only on the first consent. If you lose it (a DB bug, or a user reconnecting on another device), you need prompt=consent to get a new one. Every new consent adds a token toward the 100-per-account-per-client limit, and the oldest is revoked silently.
- Password change revokes refresh tokens that carry Gmail scopes. Treat invalid_grant as 'needs reconnect'. Stop the watch and sync jobs for that account and notify the user.
- Granular consent: users can uncheck gmail.readonly on the consent screen and still finish the flow. Always parse the token response's scope field before marking the mailbox connected.
- include_granted_scopes can pull previously granted scopes into a request, and Google 'may display' warning or error pages if the app isn't approved for all of them.
- POST /revoke removes ALL scopes for the whole project and every client in it, not just the one token.
- Pub/Sub topic and OAuth client must be in the same Cloud project, or users.watch fails.
- Notifications arrive at most once per second per user, and extras are silently dropped. Pub/Sub is at-least-once and unordered. Never process the notification's historyId directly. Always run history.list from YOUR stored cursor, dedupe by Gmail message id, and run a periodic poll as a fallback.
- Push subscription requests time out after ackDeadlineSeconds (default 10 s). A slow Vercel function will NACK and cause redelivery storms. Verify the JWT, enqueue, and return 200/204 right away.
- A Pub/Sub subscription with the default expirationPolicy is auto-deleted after 31 days of inactivity. Set an expirationPolicy with no ttl so it never expires.
- OIDC push tokens can be up to 1 hour old, so don't reject on a short iat freshness window. Check signature, aud (defaults to the endpoint URL if no audience is set), email (your push service account), email_verified and iss.
- The historyId validity window is 'at least a week' but can be 'only a few hours' in rare cases. A 404 from history.list must trigger a full resync path, and the code must be idempotent.
- Watch expires. Renew at least every 7 days, and Google recommends daily (100 units each). A watch with labelIds:[INBOX] misses newsletters that user filters auto-archive or skip-inbox. Prefer no label filter, or filter in your own code.
- History messages contain only id and threadId. messagesAdded also includes SENT and DRAFT messages, so filter by labels after fetching.
- Date literals in q (after:2026/01/01) are read as midnight PST. Use epoch seconds.
- Inline images or large parts may come back as body.attachmentId with no data. Each messages.attachments.get costs another 20 units.
- category: search and CATEGORY_* labels only reflect Gmail's inbox tabs. Users with tabs off, or with custom filters, won't have reliable categories, so combine them with header signals (List-Unsubscribe, List-Id, Precedence: bulk).
- Batch requests don't save quota (n calls count as n). Batches over 50 are 'likely to trigger rate limiting', and parts can fail individually, so retry per part. The official Node googleapis client lacks native batch support.
- The per-user concurrent request limit is shared with every other app the user has connected (their mail client, etc.), so leave headroom.
- Workspace policy bans humans reading user email without documented, specific consent. Don't log email bodies or HTML to Vercel logs, Sentry or analytics, and don't give support/admin dashboards raw email access.
- The policy REQUIRES prompt-injection protection (Model Armor 'or other') because email HTML is attacker-controlled input to Claude. The scoring pipeline must treat email content as untrusted data, never as instructions.
- Rendering emails for visual scoring loads remote images and tracking pixels, which tells senders the email was 'opened' and leaks server IPs. Render in a sandbox and consider blocking or proxying remote resources. This is an inference, not a Google rule.
- Brand verification needs authorized domains verified in Search Console, with the privacy policy on the same domain as the home page. A *.vercel.app subdomain is risky for this, so use a custom domain. This is an inference.
- The Google APIs ToS forbids 'permanent copies' of API content unless the content owner allows it, and requires data export (portability). Store bodies with user consent, apply retention limits, and delete everything on disconnect or on request.
- Microsoft refresh tokens rotate on every use, and the old one isn't revoked. Concurrent workers refreshing the same account can race, so serialize refresh per account and persist the newest token.

**Open questions (verify during build)**

- Do the new per-method costs (messages.get = 20) apply to projects grandfathered from before May 2026, or only to new projects? This matters only if the team reuses an old Cloud project, which could keep 15,000 units per user per minute and 5-unit messages.get.
- When will billing above the 80M-units-per-day threshold start, and at what price per unit? Google says 'later in 2026 with at least 90 days' notice'. Watch the tools-safety page.
- No Google page explicitly names third-party LLM APIs (Anthropic, OpenAI) as allowed subprocessors. The allowance is inferred from the Limited Use transfer clause and the approved 'generative AI summaries' use case. Expect reviewers to ask about it and disclose it plainly.
- Which CASA level (AL1 or AL2) will Google assign this app? Google decides from data sensitivity and user count. AL2 costs much more and needs read-only access to the cloud environment for review.
- Google's FAQ still mentions a free, self-scanned 'Tier 2' option, but the ADA tiering page now describes AL1 as lab-tested. Whether any free self-assessment path still exists in 2026 is unclear.
- Do Testing-mode test users count toward the 100-new-user lifetime cap of the same project? This isn't documented.
- Does the Gmail q operator header:List-Unsubscribe work in the API? It isn't documented. The documented example is only a Google Calendar header.
- Are there CATEGORY_ system label IDs for the new category:reservations and category:purchases search categories? The labels guide says its list 'isn't exhaustive'.
- Does Microsoft's publisher-verification consent block apply to personal MSA consumers, or only to users in other Entra tenants? The doc only mentions 'users in tenants that aren't the tenant where the app is registered'.
- Will Google's reviewers accept the 'Email Design Score' as part of an approved use case ('enhance the email experience' or 'reporting ... for the benefit of users')? Framing the app as inbox organization plus insights for the user, not competitive marketing research, is advisable but not guaranteed.

**Gotchas**

- Hobby cannot run sync crons more often than once a day, and firing time is imprecise (±59 min). A more frequent expression fails the deploy. Hobby is also non-commercial. For a real product, budget Pro ($20/month plus usage) from day one.
- Cron jobs can be missed, duplicated or overlap, and are never retried. Use a Redis/DB lock plus idempotent, cursor-based reconciliation (for example Gmail historyId), never 'increment' semantics.
- Cron only hits the production deployment. Sync crons never run on preview deployments, so test them by calling the route manually with the Bearer CRON_SECRET header. `next dev` and `vercel dev` have no cron support.
- Memory cannot be set per function in vercel.json with Fluid compute; it is one project-wide dashboard setting (2 GB by default, 4 GB max on Pro). A Chromium route inside the main app shares that size. Put rendering in a separate Vercel project (for example 'renderer') set to 4 GB / 2 vCPU, or offload it.
- Fluid compute runs many invocations on one instance (2 GB). Several concurrent screenshot requests that each launch Chromium (at least 1.6 GB recommended each) can run out of memory. Reuse one browser per warm instance, open one page per job, and limit throughput upstream with a queue.
- @sparticuz/chromium 153 requires Node >= 22.17. Deploying on Node 20 (also deprecated on 2026-10-01) breaks it. Pin engines.node to '24.x' (or 22.x).
- Match versions exactly: @sparticuz/chromium@153.0.0 pairs with puppeteer-core@25.11.0 (Chrome 153). The latest puppeteer-core 25.12.0 targets Chrome 154. Sparticuz can break in patch releases, so pin exact versions (no ^).
- Chromium on Lambda-style hosts ships only the Open Sans font (Latin, Greek, Cyrillic). Emoji, CJK, Arabic and similar glyphs in marketing emails will render as boxes unless you load fonts into /tmp/fonts. Without fonts, the design score's typography and visual checks will be wrong.
- The chromium-min approach of downloading a pack from your own deployment's /public URL gets 401 on Standard-Protected preview deployments. Prefer the full @sparticuz/chromium package (about 70 MB fits in 250 MB), or host the pack on a public CDN or bucket.
- If the bundler or tracer misses the Sparticuz bin files, you get errors like 'The input directory .../bin does not exist'. Next.js externalizes @sparticuz/chromium by default, but you may still need outputFileTracingIncludes for './node_modules/@sparticuz/chromium/bin/**' on the render route.
- The official Vercel Puppeteer template page still claims a 10s Hobby timeout. That is stale; Hobby is 300s under Fluid.
- The 4.5 MB request/response body limit applies to internal HTTP hops. Never POST full email HTML between functions; pass message IDs or blob pathnames.
- Vercel Queues is beta (`queue/v2beta`, `experimentalTriggers`, SDK at 0.7.0). APIs may change. It gives at-least-once, unordered delivery with no DLQ, and the default maxDeliveries is unlimited, so always set maxDeliveries and a retry callback.
- Queue messages and Workflow runs are pinned to the deployment that created them. A bug-fix deploy does not rescue in-flight work, and failing old deployments keep burning compute (up to 24h for queues by default). Cancel runs or delete the bad deployment.
- A queue delivery that times out is billed for the function's full maxDuration. Keep maxDuration tight on consumer routes.
- Workflow SDK 4.x (the stable line) always stores runs in iad1. Multi-region needs the 5.x beta. Put functions and the database in or near us-east-1 (iad1) to avoid cross-region hops on every step.
- Workflow run inputs and outputs are persisted (and readable by team owners). Do not pass raw email bodies or OAuth tokens as step arguments or return values. Pass IDs and read secrets inside steps. Payloads are capped at 50 MB, and every event and GB written is billed.
- Workflow runs above 2,000 events replay slowly, and 25,000 events per run is a hard limit. Do not make one run per user that loops over thousands of emails; fan out into child workflows or batches.
- after() and waitUntil are not durable. They are cancelled when the function hits maxDuration, and there is no retry. Use them only for best-effort side effects, not for the sync pipeline.
- Blob on Hobby includes only 2,000 advanced ops (puts) and 1 GB per month, and access is paused for 30 days once exceeded. Storing HTML snapshots or screenshots per email will exhaust it quickly. On Pro, advanced ops are rate-limited to 75/s, so throttle bulk backfills.
- A Blob store's access mode (private or public) and region cannot be changed after creation. Create a private store in the same region as your functions and DB for email-derived content.
- Private blob responses must not be cached at the CDN (no s-maxage). Do auth in the route handler next to get(), not only in proxy/middleware.
- Standard Deployment Protection leaves production public, so production webhooks work. Webhooks to preview URLs need `?x-vercel-protection-bypass=<secret>`, and rotating that secret silently breaks webhook URLs in older deployments.
- WAF challenge or deny rules and bot rules can block server-to-server webhooks such as Google Pub/Sub push. Scope challenge rules away from /api/webhooks/* (inferred from how challenges work; test it).
- WAF rate-limit counters are per region, and Hobby gets only 1 rate-limit rule per project. Use @vercel/firewall with rateLimitKey=userId, or Upstash Redis, for per-user limits.
- Enabling Standard Protection makes the generated production URL (VERCEL_URL) private. Server-side self-fetches using VERCEL_URL will get 401.
- Secret (sensitive) env vars cannot be created for the Development environment, and env var changes need a redeploy to take effect.
- Vercel KMS cannot encrypt data (signing only). Do not plan it as the envelope-encryption KMS for OAuth refresh tokens.
- The Pro function-region limit is documented inconsistently (5 on the region page, 'Up to 3' on the Fluid compute page). Do not rely on more than 3 without checking.
- When using AWS through OIDC, set AWS_REGION explicitly. Vercel sets it to the execution region, which can route KMS or S3 calls to the wrong region.
- Next.js 16 renamed middleware.ts to proxy.ts. Exclude `.well-known/workflow/*` (Workflow's internal routes) from the proxy matcher, or Workflow breaks.

**Gotchas**

- The brief's '~250 quota units/user/second' Gmail figure is OUT OF DATE for a new Google Cloud project. Projects created on or after 2026-05-01 get 6,000 units/min/user (100/s average), messages.get costs 20 units (was 5), and there is an 80M units/day/project billing threshold. Size throttles for the new numbers, or check the project's actual quota in Cloud Console.
- messages.get costs 20 units whether you ask for metadata, full or raw. Fetching metadata first and then the full message for subscription emails costs 40 units per email. One full or raw fetch (20 units) that throws away non-subscription mail is cheaper. Use messages.list q filters (e.g. category:promotions / category:updates) to cut down candidates before calling get.
- Inngest concurrency limit 1 does NOT lock a whole run. It limits executing steps, and runs that are sleeping or waiting do not count. Two sync runs for the same mailbox can interleave at step boundaries. Either use singleton, or make each step a self-contained unit (read cursor, call Gmail, upsert, compare-and-set cursor) with idempotent writes.
- Inngest throttle applies only to run starts, not steps. To make throttle match Gmail quota, cap each run's Gmail cost (e.g. one run = one batch of at most 50 messages.get = 1,000 units). A long run with many Gmail steps defeats throttling.
- Inngest rateLimit is lossy (events over the limit are dropped). Never use it for Gmail push notifications or fetch or analysis jobs. Use debounce (it keeps the last event) or throttle (it queues) instead.
- Incompatible Inngest combinations: batchEvents cannot be used with idempotency, rateLimit, cancelOn or priority. Debounce does not work with batching. Singleton cannot be used with batching and should not be combined with explicit concurrency.
- Size limits: 4MiB per step output, 32MiB total run state, 1,000 steps per function, 256KiB events on Free (3MiB on Pro), and Vercel's 4.5MB request body. Never return email HTML/MIME or screenshots from step.run or put them in events. Store them in Postgres or Blob and pass IDs.
- The 1,000-steps-per-function cap means you cannot run one step per email for a 10k-message mailbox in a single run. Fan out with step.sendEvent to per-batch or per-email functions.
- Inngest event 'id' deduplication lasts 24h and the id is GLOBAL across event types, so prefix ids (e.g. `gmail-push-${pubsubMessageId}`). Function-level idempotency also lasts only 24h. Durable deduplication must come from DB unique constraints (mailbox_id, gmail_message_id).
- Every step must finish well within Vercel maxDuration (300s on Hobby, 800s max on Pro). A timed-out step returns 504 and gets retried, so an oversized step can burn all its retries. With SDK v4 checkpointing (on by default), set checkpointing.maxRuntime 20-40% below maxDuration.
- Code outside step.run re-runs on every invocation. Put DB, Gmail and Claude calls inside steps, keep step IDs stable across deploys, and include loop indexes in IDs. Step outputs come back as JSON, so a Date becomes a string.
- Retries are per step (4 by default, so 5 attempts each), and all side effects must be idempotent. Throw NonRetriableError for permanent failures (Gmail invalid_grant or revoked token, 404 message deleted, Claude spend-cap 429 'enforced_spend_limit_reached', which has no retry-after). Throw RetryAfterError with the retry-after value on Gmail 429/403 rateLimitExceeded and Claude 429.
- On Inngest's Free plan, execution PAUSES once the 50k executions/month quota is used (there is no overage). The plan also allows only 5 concurrent steps account-wide, 100k queue depth and 24h traces. That is not enough for real backfills.
- Vercel Deployment Protection blocks Inngest's calls to /api/inngest unless you add the 'Protection Bypass for Automation' secret in Inngest's Vercel integration settings.
- Vercel function memory is project-wide (dashboard only; vercel.json memory is rejected). Hobby is fixed at 2GB/1vCPU and Pro maxes out at 4GB/2vCPU. Chromium inside the shared Inngest route can OOM, especially since Fluid compute can put several invocations in one instance. Cap render concurrency tightly or move rendering off Vercel functions.
- The Vercel Hobby plan is non-commercial only, and Hobby cron runs at most daily with ±59 min precision. Use Inngest cron triggers for watch renewal and reconciliation instead of Vercel Cron.
- Vercel Workflows has NO per-key concurrency, throttle or debounce yet (the lock/semaphore PR was still open 2026-09-15). Enforcing concurrency 1 per mailbox and per-user Gmail quotas would mean building your own Redis semaphore or token bucket. Runs stay pinned to their original deployment even after a rollback.
- Vercel Queues is still Beta (the trigger type string is 'queue/v2beta', under 'experimentalTriggers').
- Trigger.dev has no time-based rate limiting (only concurrency), so the Gmail units/min and Claude RPM/TPM limits would have to be enforced in your own code. Its docs limits page is stale (10/25/100 concurrency, raised to 20/50/200 in Nov 2025).
- Upstash Workflow flowControl is fixed per run and inherited by every step. You cannot give one step (e.g. the Claude call) a different limit unless you split it out with context.invoke or context.call.
- The Claude spend-cap 429 looks like a rate limit (type rate_limit_error) but retries never succeed. Check error.details.error_code before you retry.
- Inngest cron near DST changes can misfire, so write cron expressions in UTC (TZ=UTC ...).
- Inngest docs contradict themselves: the usage-limits page lists a 'Basic' plan and 'Pro 200+' concurrent steps, while the pricing page shows Free/Pro/Business with Pro at 100 included. Budget against the pricing page.

**Gotchas**

- Next.js 16: middleware.ts is deprecated and proxy.ts always runs on Node.js. Do not do DB or session work in proxy for every request. Use the optimistic `getSessionCookie()` redirect there and do the real auth check in the Data Access Layer and in every Server Action or route handler.
- Every Server Action is a public POST endpoint, and a page-level auth check does not cover it. Check the session and resource ownership (userId / mailboxId) inside each action, or you get cross-tenant IDOR.
- `'use cache'` entries are shared across requests. Never read per-user data inside a cached scope unless it is keyed by an argument. `cookies()`/`headers()` inside a cached helper can pass `next build` and then fail under `next start`. The simplest safe start for an all-dynamic authenticated dashboard is to leave `cacheComponents` off.
- TypeScript 7.0.2 is npm latest, but typescript-eslint (used by eslint-config-next 16.3.6) requires typescript <6.1.0. Pin typescript ~6.0.3, or the lint toolchain breaks.
- Prisma's `latest` dist-tag for the CLI points to 8.0.0-rc.17 while @prisma/client latest is 7.10.0. Better Auth's peer range stops at prisma ^7, so a plain `npm i prisma` installs an RC. That is one more reason to use Drizzle.
- Drizzle docs now default to `@rc` (v1) install commands and syntax. If you install stable drizzle-orm 0.45.x, follow v0 docs: RQB v1 and the journal.json migration layout. Do not mix the two.
- Run migrations against DATABASE_URL_UNPOOLED. Neon's PgBouncer transaction mode breaks SET, session advisory locks, LISTEN/NOTIFY and SQL-level PREPARE. Use transaction-scoped `pg_advisory_xact_lock` for per-mailbox sync locks.
- With Fluid compute, create the pg Pool at module scope, call `attachDatabasePool(pool)`, and keep pool `max` small (single digits). Neon allows 10,000 client connections through the pooler, but at 0.25 CU there are only 104 max_connections.
- Neon Free has 0.5 GB storage per project. Thousands of raw emails per user will not fit in Postgres. Store raw MIME/HTML and screenshots in private Vercel Blob (or R2/S3) and keep metadata, features and scores in Postgres.
- Better Auth stores OAuth tokens in plaintext unless `account.encryptOAuthTokens: true`. Even then idToken stays plaintext and the key is derived from BETTER_AUTH_SECRET. Rotating that secret without `secrets: [{version, value}]` makes stored tokens undecryptable.
- Setting Better Auth Google `accessType: 'offline'` plus `prompt: 'consent'` on the provider forces a consent screen and mints a new refresh token on every login. Google keeps only 100 per account per client and silently drops the oldest. Pass `additionalParams: { access_type: 'offline', prompt: 'consent' }` only on the one-time 'Connect Gmail' flow.
- In Better Auth, every account linked via linkSocial becomes a login method for that user. Linking a second Gmail mailbox lets that Google identity sign in to the app. Linking a mailbox whose email differs from the user's email also needs `allowDifferentEmails: true`.
- Better Auth `getAccessToken` refreshes only when the token is less than 5s from expiry and has no concurrency lock. Long sync jobs should re-fetch or refresh the token between batches and serialize refresh per mailbox.
- Better Auth's default rate-limit storage is in-memory, which does nothing on serverless. Set `rateLimit.storage: 'database'` or 'secondary-storage'. On Vercel use `advanced.ipAddress.ipAddressHeaders: ['x-real-ip']` (or 'x-vercel-forwarded-for'). Server-side `auth.api` calls bypass the limiter.
- The Better Auth CLI package is now `auth` (`npx auth@latest generate`). `@better-auth/cli` is frozen at 1.4.21 and generates stale schemas.
- Google OAuth apps in 'Testing' status with an External user type get refresh tokens that die after 7 days. Syncs will break a week after launch unless the app is published and verified.
- Sentry @sentry/nextjs v11 collects cookies, HTTP bodies, genAI prompts and outputs, and DB query data by default when `dataCollection` is unset. For an app that processes users' email this leaks email content and PII to Sentry. Set `dataCollection` restrictively and scrub in `beforeSend`. Also note the import change to '@sentry/nextjs/config'.
- Vercel Workflows and Inngest persist step inputs and outputs (Vercel team owners can read decrypted run data). Pass IDs such as messageId and mailboxId between steps, never email bodies or OAuth tokens.
- puppeteer-core latest (25.12.0) targets Chrome 154, but the newest @sparticuz/chromium is 153. Pin puppeteer-core 25.11.x. Also add `serverExternalPackages: ['@sparticuz/chromium']`, or bundling breaks the binary path ('/var/task/bin does not exist').
- Serverless Chromium ships only Open Sans. Email screenshots will substitute Arial, Helvetica, Georgia, CJK and emoji fonts, which skews typography and layout scoring. Provision fonts into /tmp/fonts (in fonts.conf) before launch, or include them via outputFileTracingIncludes.
- DOMPurify on the server must use an up-to-date jsdom (30.x needs Node ^22.22.2/^24.15). happy-dom is explicitly unsafe, and linkedom is not a vetted security boundary. postal-mime output is not sanitized.
- Fetching remote images or links from emails server-side causes three problems. It triggers sender open/click tracking (fake opens). It is an SSRF vector (block private IPs, limit redirects and size). And GET/HEAD on unsubscribe links can unsubscribe the user, which RFC 8058 exists to prevent. Skip List-Unsubscribe URLs and known unsubscribe paths in broken-link checks.
- shadcn now defaults to Base UI (`render` prop, not `asChild`), and its data-table uses TanStack Table v9 (`useTable` plus `tableFeatures`). v8 or Radix code from tutorials or LLMs will not type-check against freshly generated components. Pick one primitive library at `init` and stick to it.
- Vercel Hobby is non-commercial only, has 4 Active CPU-hours per month, and allows cron only once per day with ±59 min precision. Background mailbox sync for multiple users needs Pro ($20/seat).
- Next.js 16.3.6 fixes a critical next/og RCE (>=16.2.0 <16.3.6), and 16.3.7 ships 2026-09-30 with 9 more fixes, 1 critical. Budget an upgrade right away. Never deploy 16.2.x or 16.3.0 to 16.3.5.
- Vitest does not support async Server Components. Test them with Playwright E2E, and unit-test pure scoring and parsing modules with Vitest.

- Should Vercel Workflows or Inngest orchestrate the background sync? Both are verified as viable. Deciding needs the other research streams' numbers: Gmail API quota and throughput, and the expected fan-out per sync.
- Should mailbox connections live in Better Auth's `account` table (fastest MVP) or in a separate `mailbox_connection` table (recommended)? This is a product decision: can a user connect more than one mailbox, or a mailbox different from their login account?

- Which Claude model to use per task (per-email classification versus screenshot design critique) is the product owner's call. Current prices are verified; quality per task needs a small eval.

**Gotchas**

- Test tokens die after 7 days: refresh tokens for an OAuth client in 'Testing' publishing status expire in 7 days, so long-running sync tests fail until the app is in production status.
- srcdoc iframes inherit the parent CSP. A strict nonce CSP silently strips email inline styles and blocks proxy images unless the parent CSP is loosened. Serve emails from a separate cookie-free domain instead.
- sandbox without allow-popups makes target=_blank links do nothing. Adding allow-scripts together with allow-same-origin cancels the sandbox. A no-script, opaque-origin frame cannot auto-size.
- DOMPurify's default allowlist keeps <style>, <form>, <input>, <button>, <select>, <textarea>, <video> and <audio>, and never sanitizes CSS values. Leading <style> blocks can be dropped unless FORCE_BODY is set.
- An image proxy on your main domain that serves sender SVG or HTML becomes stored XSS when someone opens the proxy URL directly. Host the proxy on a cookie-free domain, send nosniff plus CSP 'default-src none; sandbox', and reject or rasterize SVG.
- An unsigned image proxy is an open proxy and an SSRF amplifier. HMAC-sign proxy URLs (message id, url, expiry) and still run full SSRF checks inside the proxy.
- Fetching images at ingest marks every newsletter as 'opened' for the sender and changes the user's engagement signals. Fetching links can confirm double opt-ins, unsubscribe the user through GET body links, or register clicks; HEAD often counts as a click too.
- Sender images are attacker-controlled input to sharp/libvips/libheif and to Chromium's decoders. Aug 2026 saw an AVIF RCE in Next.js Image Optimization via libheif. Do not route sender images through next/image optimization.
- @sparticuz/chromium's default args include --disable-web-security, --allow-running-insecure-content and --no-sandbox. Filter the first two, and never give the render function DB, KMS or Gmail credentials.
- setJavaScriptEnabled(false) only applies from the next navigation, so call it before setContent.
- Native fetch (undici) ignores http.Agent, so request-filtering-agent does nothing with fetch(). Resolving and validating first, then calling fetch(), is a DNS-rebinding hole.
- request-filtering-agent below 2.0.0 let HTTPS to 127.0.0.1 through (CVE-2025-57814), and below 3.2.1 can crash the process (CVE-2026-62985). ssrf-req-filter was last released in May 2024.
- ipaddr.js accepts octal, hex and 3-part IPv4 ('0177.0.0.1', '2130706433'-style inputs). Check the resolved address with range() after process(), not the literal string.
- SET LOCAL outside an explicit transaction is a silent no-op. Neon HTTP autocommits each query. A plain SET through Neon's PgBouncer (transaction mode) can leak one tenant's context into another's session.
- Drizzle neon-http: db.transaction() throws. Use db.batch() (a single transaction) with set_config(..., true) as the first statement, or use the WebSocket Pool driver.
- Table owners and BYPASSRLS roles skip RLS. Neon's default owner role and Console-created roles (neon_superuser, which carries BYPASSRLS) are wrong for runtime queries. Use a SQL-created NOBYPASSRLS role plus FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY.
- Unique and foreign-key checks bypass RLS and can reveal that a row exists in another tenant (e.g. a unique constraint on gmail_message_id without tenant_id). Make uniqueness per tenant.
- Server Actions are public POST endpoints. A page-level auth check does not protect actions defined on that page, and the Origin check does not cover route.ts handlers.
- Proxy/middleware-only authorization has been bypassed repeatedly: CVE-2025-29927 plus five proxy bypasses in May 2026 and one in July 2026. Authorize in the Data Access Layer and with RLS.
- Nonce CSP forces dynamic rendering: no ISR, no PPR, no CDN caching.
- Auth.js's default session maxAge is 30 days. CASA 2.2.3 requires non-revocable stateless tokens to expire within 24 hours.
- CASA 6.5.1: never log credentials. Also keep email bodies, subjects and addresses out of Vercel logs, Sentry breadcrumbs and LLM tracing tools. Logging them sends Gmail data to extra third parties and exposes it to human reading.
- Limited Use covers derived data too. Building a cross-user 'design benchmark' or training or fine-tuning a general scoring model on users' mailbox emails is prohibited beyond per-user personalization, even for bulk newsletters.
- Covered LLM models and the Anthropic Files API retain data (30 days or until deleted) even if you have ZDR. Stay on the stateless Messages API and disclose retention in the privacy policy.
- Email content is a prompt-injection vector against your classifier: newsletters can contain hidden text that tells the model to score a certain way. Google's policy now explicitly requires prompt-injection protection.
- Vercel Preview deployments are internet-reachable. Restrict the OIDC trust policy to environment:production for production KMS keys, and use separate keys and DBs for preview.
- The KMS encryption context appears in plaintext in CloudTrail. Put only opaque UUIDs in it, never email addresses.
- Google's assurance level is dynamic. Crossing user-count thresholds can move you from AL1 to AL2 at the next annual cycle, which roughly multiplies the cost by 5 to 8.

**Security checklist (from research)**

_MVP must-haves_

- Upgrade to next>=16.3.6 or 15.5.26. Add Dependabot/Renovate plus osv-scanner or npm audit in CI.
- Separate login (openid/email/profile) from the Gmail connect step. Use PKCE and state, and check the granted 'scope' after the callback.
- Encrypt refresh tokens with AWS KMS or GCP KMS through Vercel OIDC. Bind them with an encryption context or AAD of user_id and google_sub. No long-lived cloud keys in env vars.
- Put a DAL on every Server Action and route handler for authn, authz and ownership checks, and validate inputs with Zod. Do not authorize in proxy.ts alone.
- Postgres: tenant_id/user_id on every table, a SQL-created NOBYPASSRLS runtime role, ENABLE + FORCE RLS, set_config(...,true) inside a transaction (Pool, or neon-http batch), and a CI test for cross-tenant access.
- Sanitize email HTML on the server with DOMPurify >=3.4.16 and the latest jsdom, plus a strict FORBID list and CSS url() rewriting.
- Render previews in <iframe sandbox> with no allow-scripts and no allow-same-origin, a CSP that limits img-src to the proxy, and referrerpolicy=no-referrer. Rewrite links to target=_blank rel=noopener noreferrer through a signed interstitial.
- Build an HMAC-signed image proxy with full SSRF protection (resolve and pin the IP, per-hop redirect checks, byte and time caps, image content-type allowlist, no SVG/AVIF/HEIF), and fetch only on user action.
- Keep link checking OFF, or DNS-only, by default. Never touch List-Unsubscribe URLs or unsubscribe/confirm/tracking links.
- Headless Chromium in a separate secret-less function: filter the web-security flags, JS disabled, interception that allows only data: URIs, offline mode, and timeouts.
- Security headers (HSTS, frame-ancestors 'none', nosniff, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy, COOP) and __Host- HttpOnly Secure SameSite=Lax session cookies.
- Rate limiting (@upstash/ratelimit or Vercel WAF) on auth, sync, proxy and LLM endpoints.
- LLM calls: stateless Messages API, no tools, delimited input, schema-validated output, no logging of email content or prompts.
- Account deletion that revokes the Google token and purges data. Keep no email content in logs, Sentry or analytics.

_Before Google verification / CASA_

- Privacy policy and homepage: add the Limited Use disclosure statement, AI/ML non-training commitment, list of subprocessors (Anthropic, Vercel, Neon, AWS/GCP) with retention (Anthropic 30 days, or ZDR), human-access policy, and retention and deletion terms.
- Document the prompt-injection protection (Model Armor or your own controls), as the Workspace policy requires.
- Map every CASA v2.1.1 item to evidence. Key items: 2.2.1 logout invalidates sessions; 2.2.3 24h cap on stateless tokens (switch Auth.js to database sessions or a short JWT maxAge); 3.1.4 IDOR tests; 3.1.5 CSRF; 3.3.1 MFA on admin surfaces; 5.1.2 redirect interstitial; 5.1.5 SSRF tests; 6.3.1 Origin not used for authn/authz; 6.4.1 subdomain takeover; 6.5.1 no credentials in logs; 6.6.1 Clear-Site-Data on logout; 6.7.1 secrets and tokens in KMS.
- Run your own SAST, DAST (authenticated ZAP or Burp using the ADA Burp config) and dependency scans. Fix all High/Critical findings before engaging the lab, since the self-scan process is deprecated but still useful for readiness.
- Pick an ADA-authorized lab that is authorized for CASA (e.g. TAC Security, NetSentries, DEKRA, Bishop Fox, Leviathan). Budget about $700–$2k for AL1 and $5k–$10k for AL2, and 2–6 weeks including remediation.
- Record a demo video of the OAuth flow and scope justification for Google. Show that each feature uses gmail.readonly in a way visible in the UI.
- Retention policy enforced by a scheduled purge job. Audit logging of security events. RISC integration for token revocation events.
- Separate usercontent domain for email previews and the image proxy, with its own CSP header including the sandbox directive.
- Move the render worker to Vercel Sandbox with an egress firewall, or a gVisor/Firecracker-isolated service, if you ever render remote resources.
- Put an incident-response runbook in place, including GDPR's 72-hour breach notification and secret rotation. Rotate all secrets after any critical framework CVE window, as with React2Shell.

_Nice to have_

- Request Anthropic Zero Data Retention for your organization and avoid Covered Models that require 30-day retention.
- Use envelope encryption with @aws-crypto/client-node or the KMS hierarchical keyring to cut KMS calls. Re-encrypt tokens on refresh to migrate key versions.
- Try Next.js experimental SRI (hash-based CSP) to keep static rendering with a strict CSP.
- Add per-tenant data-encryption keys for stored email bodies, so deleting a tenant's key crypto-shreds their data.
- Inline and rasterize all remote CSS and images into data URIs at render time, like Talos' privacy proxy, and serve previews as the sandboxed PNG with the sanitized HTML as a fallback.
- Offer an interstitial for external links with a domain reputation check and a warning for lookalike or IDN domains.
- Run a bug bounty or yearly third-party pentest beyond CASA, and consider SOC 2 later.
- Add CSP violation reporting (report-to) on both the app and usercontent domains.
- Give users a UI to review, export and delete stored emails, and a per-sender opt-out from analysis.

**Gotchas**

- Pass/fail accessibility scoring would fail almost everything: EMC 2026 found 99.88% of emails have Serious/Critical defects. Binary gates give no spread between emails. Use graded, capped penalties per check and show percentile bands next to the absolute score.
- body-dir is missing in 97.41% of emails and axe/Parcel rate it 'Serious', but for readers using left-to-right languages the practical impact is small. Weight it lightly or it flattens the score.
- Gmail's 102KB clip limit is not documented by Google, and clipping has been reported at about 99KB. Measure the UTF-8 byte length of the DECODED text/html part (after quoted-printable/base64). The Gmail API returns bodies base64url-encoded, which inflates length by about 33%. If the unsubscribe link or postal address falls after the clip point, flag 'unsubscribe hidden in Gmail'.
- Gmail keeps only the first 16KB of <style>; rules after that are silently dropped (caniemail/email-bugs, not Google docs).
- Headless Chromium renders HTML more permissively than Gmail or classic Outlook: it supports flex, grid, base64 images, <picture>, SVG and @font-face. Treat screenshot metrics as a best-case render. Add a separate compatibility-risk check based on caniemail, e.g. flex/grid and background-image in Outlook classic, and @font-face, base64 images, <picture> and <video> in Gmail.
- Loading remote images to take a screenshot fires the sender's open-tracking pixels, so the sender sees a fake 'open' from your server IP. Tell the user about this, block known tracking pixels (for example 1x1 images or known tracker domains), or render without images first.
- Automated link checking can hit unsubscribe URLs, double-opt-in confirmations, magic-login links and one-time tokens, which unsubscribes users or uses up tokens. Never GET or POST List-Unsubscribe URLs or URLs matching unsubscribe/confirm/verify/login/token patterns. Many ESP click-tracking redirects also log a 'click'. Consider resolving DNS or checking only the domain, or explicitly asking the user before checking links.
- Inline images referenced as cid: must be resolved from MIME parts or attachments before rendering, or they look broken and unfairly count as 'missing images'.
- Contrast cannot be computed from CSS when text sits on a background image or gradient. Sample rendered pixels behind the text box, or mark the check 'needs review' as axe does, instead of reporting a pass or fail.
- Large-text rule: 24px, or 18.66px AND bold (font-weight >= 700). Never round contrast ratios; 4.499 fails.
- WCAG 2.5.8 exempts inline links in sentences and targets that meet the 24px-circle spacing rule. Apply target-size checks only to buttons and standalone links.
- Don't cite WCAG 1.4.12 as 'line-height must be 1.5'. It tests robustness to user overrides. The 1.5 figure in 1.4.8 is AAA. Litmus recommends 1.5x but its own code example uses about 1.29x.
- Dark mode: Gmail supports neither prefers-color-scheme nor color-scheme (its apps accept only 'light only'). Not having dark-mode CSS is not a defect for Gmail readers. Score dark-mode RISK instead, e.g. pure #000/#FFF, dark-on-transparent logos, and text baked into images, by simulating full and partial inversion.
- The 60/40 image-to-text rule is a myth. SpamAssassin's HTML_IMAGE_RATIO rules score 0.001. Penalize image-only or nearly image-only emails (small live-text length plus images, per HTML_IMAGE_ONLY_*) and key messages or CTAs that exist only as images, not the ratio.
- Marketing stats such as 'single CTA +371% clicks' and 'buttons +28% CTR' have no published method. Don't quote them in user-facing explanations. Research on choice overload is mixed (the 2010 meta-analysis found a mean effect near zero).
- Reinecke's complexity and colorfulness models were trained on 450 WEBSITES viewed for 500ms, not emails. Transfer to 600px single-column emails is unvalidated. Centered single-column emails will score near the maximum on Ngo symmetry/balance, so those metrics barely separate emails.
- Hasler colorfulness implementations differ: the paper uses signed opponent channels, while popular code uses absolute values. Fix one formula and the 0-255 scaling. The widely repeated category cut-offs (15/33/45/59/82/109) are unverified.
- Flesch formulas are English-only and break on bullets, fragments, CTA labels and footers. Run them only on body prose above a minimum word count, and skip non-English emails, or use language-specific formulas. Litmus's '60-70' target refers to Flesch Reading Ease, not the FK grade level.
- Open rates are unreliable because of Apple MPP (about 55-60% of opens per Litmus). Never validate subject-line or design scores against open rates.
- Transactional mail (receipts, password resets) is exempt from Gmail's one-click unsubscribe requirement, and CAN-SPAM rules depend on 'primary purpose'. Classify the message type before applying unsubscribe or postal-address penalties.
- Gmail FAQ: when the header supports one-click, the body unsubscribe link does not have to be one-click. Check that a body link is visible, not that it is one-click.
- Google Limited Use: users' Gmail content cannot be used to train or improve a generalized model, including learned score weights across users. Calibration data must come from somewhere else, and human reading of user emails needs explicit per-message consent.
- Really Good Emails content is 'All rights reserved' (BEE Content Design). Don't scrape it as a calibration set without permission.
- caniemail's 'Outlook Windows' entries cover only classic versions up to 2019; the 'windows-mail' key is the separate Windows Mail app. New Outlook for Windows behaves like Outlook.com, so map clients carefully.
- The Microsoft high-volume sender post contains conflicting text (Junk first vs reject). The April 29, 2025 update says messages are rejected with 550 5.7.515. Describe enforcement cautiously.

**Gotchas**

- Never GET a List-Unsubscribe HTTPS URL, and never GET links from an email server-side (link checkers, redirect resolvers, headless renderers). RFC 8058 exists because automated GETs unsubscribe people. Tracked links and pixels will also record fake clicks and opens and confirm the address is live. A 'broken links' check must skip unsubscribe, preference-center and click-tracking URLs, or run only with explicit user action.
- When rendering emails for design scoring (screenshots or vision), block remote requests to known tracker hosts and to detected pixels, or stub them out. Otherwise our servers fire open-tracking for every email analyzed and leak the fact that it was processed.
- One-click unsubscribe on the user's behalf requires all of the following: explicit user consent per action; a trusted Authentication-Results showing dkim=pass; a DKIM-Signature whose h= covers both list-unsubscribe and list-unsubscribe-post; a POST with body List-Unsubscribe=One-Click and no cookies or auth; and treating any 3xx as failure, since the RFC forbids redirects. Harden against SSRF: https only, resolve DNS and block private, loopback and link-local ranges including 169.254.169.254, short timeouts, capped response size, no redirect following. Serverless functions on Vercel have open egress.
- List-Unsubscribe and List-Id are not proof of marketing. GitHub notifications, forums (Mailman, Google Groups) and many transactional ESP sends carry them. Their absence is not proof of personal or transactional mail either: senders under 5,000/day aren't bound by Gmail's rules, and transactional mail is exempt from one-click.
- Feedback-ID means 'ESP or FBL participant', not 'marketing'. SES stamps it on every message, including password resets.
- Precedence is non-standard (RFC 3834). Weight it lightly. Auto-Submitted: auto-replied is an out-of-office or auto-reply, so exclude it from subscriptions.
- Trust only Authentication-Results from our own mailbox provider's authserv-id: the topmost header with authserv-id mx.google.com for Gmail. Forwarded messages and spoofers can embed fake 'dkim=pass' headers. Microsoft uses the non-standard dmarc=bestguesspass. DMARC is now RFC 9989 (May 2026): pct is gone and np, psd and t are new.
- A BIMI-Location or BIMI-Indicator header supplied by the sender is meaningless or spoofed, because only the receiver MTA may insert it. Don't turn it into a 'verified brand' badge without our own DNS and certificate validation.
- Header parsing traps: folded lines; several instances of the same header; RFC 2047 encoded-words; comments in parentheses; whitespace inside <...> that RFC 2369 says to ignore; mailto URIs with ?subject=&body=; header names that are case-insensitive. The Gmail API headers array can repeat names. Don't assume values arrive decoded: format=raw plus a MIME parser such as postal-mime (MIT-0) or mailparser (MIT) gives full fidelity.
- ESP fingerprints disappear when senders use branded link domains, a custom Return-Path or their own DKIM. Mailgun headers can be stripped with o:suppress-headers=all. Platforms such as Braze sit on SES, SendGrid or SparkPost, so an 'SES' fingerprint may really be Braze. Keep platform and MTA as separate fields and allow 'unknown'.
- Postmark is not always transactional (it has Broadcast streams). Klaviyo's DKIM selector (km*/kt*/ks*) is a cleaner marketing-vs-transactional hint than the ESP name.
- License traps: the Disconnect email tracker lists and DuckDuckGo blocklists are CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (no commercial use). The Ugly Email tracker list has no license. Inbox Zero is AGPL-3.0 with a commercial-monetization restriction. SimpleLogin is AGPL-3.0, FairEmail GPL-3.0, AdGuard filters GPL or LGPL. Don't bundle any of these in a paid SaaS without legal review. Trocker (Apache-2.0) is safe with attribution.
- Gmail category labels are user-editable, so they mix Gmail's ML with user moves. category:purchases and category:reservations are search operators, not label IDs. The q parameter doesn't work with the gmail.metadata scope, and gmail.metadata is Restricted anyway, so it saves no verification effort.
- Google Workspace policy forbids training or improving AI/ML models on user data beyond that user's personalized model. Don't pool user corrections into a global model and don't fine-tune on user emails. Calibrate on internal or consented data.
- JSON-LD for Gmail markup and Promotions annotations sits in <head> <script> tags. Most HTML sanitizers remove these, so extract structured data from the raw HTML before sanitizing. Markup can also be stale or wrong: validate the date format and compare expiry with the Date header.
- Tracking-pixel detection by width/height alone misses pixels sized by CSS classes or with no dimensions. Combine size, style (display:none, opacity:0, max-width:1px), URL path (/open, /track/open, /wf/open) and host lists. Gmail proxies images and Apple Mail Privacy Protection pre-fetches them, so open-tracking is noisy. Report 'contains tracking', never 'was opened'.
- Deal extraction traps: relative expiries ('ends tonight', '48 hours only') must be anchored to the email's Date header, not processing time; the timezone is often unstated; US vs EU date order (10/05); 'up to X% off' overstates the offer; stacked offers ('extra 20% off sale prices'); exclusions; codes that exist only in images (alt text, or vision/OCR needed); per-recipient unique codes (private, never aggregate or share across users); countdown-timer images that re-render live; 'no code needed'; order, tracking and reference numbers that look like coupon codes.
- Language detectors are unreliable on short text (franc returns 'und' under 10 characters and is 'easily confused on small samples'). CLD3 is archived. eld's database uses 37–138 MB RAM, which affects serverless cold start and memory. Run detection on stripped visible body text, not subject lines alone.
- Microsoft BCL, Forefront and Promotions-tag headers are documented for Microsoft 365 cloud mailboxes. Don't assume consumer Outlook.com carries them until tested.

**Open questions (verify during build)**

- Does the Gmail API's messages.list q honor category:purchases and category:reservations the way the web search box does? These are not system labels. Needs a live test with a real account.
- Does Gmail keep receiver-inserted BIMI-Location or BIMI-Indicator headers in messages returned by the Gmail API (format=raw or full)? Not documented.
- Exact delivered-mail header markers for Mailchimp (X-MC-User), Brevo (X-Mailin-EID), Klaviyo, Iterable, Customer.io and Salesforce Marketing Cloud (e.g., X-SFMC-Stack) could not be verified in official docs. Confirm them against real sample messages before hardcoding.
- Does the Gmail API return RFC 2047-encoded header values decoded or raw in payload.headers? Decide whether to always use format=raw plus our own MIME parser.
- Do consumer Outlook.com mailboxes carry X-Microsoft-Antispam BCL, X-Forefront-Antispam-Report and the Promotions tag the way Microsoft 365 does? Matters only once Outlook support starts.
- Can Disconnect's Email tracker list be licensed commercially, or should we maintain our own list from scratch?
- Is Gmail's CATEGORY_* labeling applied when a user has inbox tabs turned off? It affects how much weight category labels deserve. Unverified.
- Which Anthropic API data-retention and zero-retention options apply to email content sent for classification? Belongs to the LLM/security research track, and must line up with Google Limited Use disclosures.
- The proposed heuristic weights and thresholds are design proposals, not verified facts. They need calibration on an internally labeled corpus (our own or consented mailboxes, never pooled user data, per Google policy).

**Gotchas**

- Hobby limits apply to the whole Hobby team, not to each project. Splitting the app into app, viewer and renderer projects adds no capacity. Running extra Hobby accounts to multiply quotas is 'circumventing' limits under fair use.
- Hobby usage is counted over a rolling 30-day window, not a calendar month. Budgets must be enforced every day (limit × safety factor ÷ 30), not reset on the 1st.
- Going over a limit can pause the whole team: production returns 503 DEPLOYMENT_PAUSED with 'Upgrade to Pro to resume service'. Community reports (Sep 2026) show teams still paused after 30+ days, with support declining to unblock. Treat any breach as possibly a month or more of downtime. The self-serve 3x unblock can be used only once.
- Memory is fixed at 2 GB on Hobby, so every second an instance is alive costs 2 GB-seconds, even while waiting on the Claude API or Gmail. That makes 360 GB-h only 180 instance-hours a month. Long synchronous AI calls use up memory, not CPU. Use the Anthropic Message Batches API and poll instead of holding a function open.
- No per-route switch for Fluid in-instance concurrency. Several Chromium renders routed to one 2 GB instance can crash with out-of-memory. Serialize renders in application code (single consumer, one browser, one page).
- Workflows 5 has not shipped per-key concurrency, locks, semaphores or rate limits. PR #4174 is only a plan document (2026-09-15), and 5.x is still beta (5.0.0-beta.57). Build per-mailbox mutual exclusion with Postgres advisory locks or lease rows.
- Queues push consumers have no concurrency cap in vercel.json (only the poll-mode HTTP header Vqs-Max-Concurrency), and maxDeliveries defaults to unlimited. Always set maxDeliveries and a retry callback. A timed-out delivery is billed for the function's full maxDuration.
- Queue messages and Workflow runs stay pinned to the deployment that created them. A delayed-message 'tick chain' or sleep-loop scheduler keeps running old code after a redeploy, and a new deploy's daily cron can start a second chain. Stamp each tick with an idempotency key per slot plus a DB lease, and end the chain when VERCEL_DEPLOYMENT_ID is not the current production deployment.
- Queue delivery is at-least-once. Without idempotency, one duplicate tick in a self-rescheduling chain doubles the chain and can grow until it hits limits.
- Blob on Hobby includes only 2,000 advanced ops (puts) a month. Browsing the store in the dashboard also counts as advanced ops. Going over locks you out of Blob for 30 days. The two Blob rate limits disagree (900 vs 1,500 per minute advanced).

- Runtime logs are kept for only 1 hour, and Log Drains need Pro. Without your own PII-free error table, failed overnight cron runs cannot be diagnosed.
- The daily Hobby cron fires anywhere in the scheduled hour, can be skipped or duplicated, is never retried and only hits production. Near-real-time sync cannot depend on it.
- Vercel Authentication (Deployment Protection) admits only members of the Hobby team, which means only the owner. Friends can never pass it. Production must stay public (Standard Protection) with auth inside the app. Standard Protection also makes VERCEL_URL private, so self-fetches to it get 401.
- vercel.app is on the Public Suffix List. The viewer on another *.vercel.app project is cross-site, so it gets no app session cookies (third-party cookies are blocked). Authenticate viewer loads with short-lived signed URL tokens, never cookies.
- Hobby accounts are opted in to Vercel AI model training by default. Opt out on day one in Team Settings → Data Preferences.
- The Hobby terms let Vercel 'shut down and terminate projects... without notice for any reason'. Keep the database outside Vercel and the code in git so you can move.
- Do not use next/image for remote email images. Hobby includes only 5K image transformations, and it widens the SSRF surface.
- @sparticuz/chromium 153 needs Node ^22.17 or >=24. Vercel deprecates Node 20 on 2026-10-01. Pin engines.node to 24.x and pin puppeteer-core to exactly 25.11.0 (25.12.0 targets Chrome 154).

The design must switch to coarse steps.

**Gotchas**

- Vercel Hobby allowances are per account, not per project. A separate 'renderer' project uses the same 4 Active CPU-hours as the app, and going over pauses Functions for 30 days, which takes the whole app offline (sync, UI, webhooks). Never let Chromium run on Hobby functions without a hard self-budget, and preferably not at all.
- Chromium runs as child processes. process.cpuUsage() in Node does not include their CPU, but Vercel's Active CPU probably does (inferred). For self-budgeting, count wall-time × vCPUs as a conservative upper bound.
- Fluid compute packs concurrent invocations into one 2 GB instance. Two concurrent Chromium launches can run out of memory, so enforce one render per instance (semaphore) and concurrency 1 upstream.
- Browser Run bills and limits session wall time, including idle keep_alive. Do not keep sessions warm between batches, and close the session before CPU-heavy post-processing. Free sessions also close after 60 s idle, and new browsers are limited to 1 every 20 s.
- Browser Run Quick Actions cache results for 5 s by default (set cacheTTL: 0 for mail), cannot use Guardrails, and cannot return computed styles or text boxes. Only CDP sessions can produce the DESIGN-SCORE §2 features.
- The Browser Run docs do not list which CDP domains are supported. DOMSnapshot.captureSnapshot, Emulation.setDeviceMetricsOverride, Emulation.setScriptExecutionDisabled/setJavaScriptEnabled(false), Fetch interception and Runtime.evaluate for axe with JS off all need a day-1 spike.
- Workers Free (for the binding route) is limited to 10 ms CPU per request, 128 MB and 50 subrequests. Do not process DOMSnapshots inside a Worker; drive CDP from the Vercel step (or from inside a Vercel Sandbox so the CPU comes from the Sandbox pool).
- Chromium version and fonts differ between hosts (Cloudflare-managed Chromium vs a pinned Playwright Chromium in Sandbox vs users' browsers), so scores can drift slightly when falling back. Store renderer_host and chromium_version with the features, and inject the same metric-compatible fonts (Arimo, Tinos, Cousine, Gelasio as data-URI @font-face aliases) on every host.
- Vercel Sandbox is persistent by default. Automatic snapshots would store email content and use up Hobby's 15 GB lifetime snapshot storage. Always pass persistent:false and networkPolicy:'deny-all', and start from one base snapshot with expiration 0.
- Vercel Sandbox provisioned memory has a 1-minute minimum per sandbox, and Hobby allows 5,000 creations a month. Batch many emails per sandbox rather than one sandbox per email. Hobby sessions last at most 45 minutes.
- Vercel Sandbox's 30-day pause runs 'from first use', not the calendar month. Budget on a rolling 30-day window.
- Cloud Run's free egress (1 GiB/month from North America) runs out long before its compute allowance if the renderer returns screenshots. Return compact features plus small WebP thumbnails, or you will pay cents per GB. A Chromium container image also usually exceeds Artifact Registry's free 0.5 GB.
- Google Cloud budgets never cap spending. The documented kill switch disables billing for the whole project, stops all services and can irretrievably delete resources, and billing data lags by hours.
- Oracle Always Free A1 was quietly halved in 2026 (now 2 OCPU/12 GB). An idle render VM can meet the reclamation test (7-day p95 CPU, network and memory all under 20%). Accounts idle 30 days can be suspended. Prepaid and virtual cards are rejected at sign-up.
- The Trigger.dev Free concurrency figure conflicts (pricing page says 20, limits doc says 10), and whether a card is needed is not stated officially. Trigger stores run payloads and outputs, so never pass email HTML as a payload.
- Browserless Free sessions are capped at 2 minutes and 2 concurrent, units are 30 s blocks per connection, and exhaustion behaviour is undocumented.
- Client-side Option A (same-origin frame, no scripts) puts hostile mail on the session origin, and a srcdoc frame inherits the app CSP, which forces style-src 'unsafe-inline' on the app. Both are security regressions against v1.1 §11.5.
- Client-side results are not reproducible across users (OS fonts, engine, DPR), work only while the app is open, and cannot use CDP DOMSnapshot. html2canvas has had no release since January 2022, and HTML-in-Canvas is still a Chrome origin trial (148-150).
- Every third-party renderer (Cloudflare, Browserless, Trigger.dev, Google, AWS) is a sub-processor of Gmail-derived content under restricted-scope Limited Use. List it in the privacy policy, even for an unverified invite-only app.
- GitHub Actions (terms of service), Hugging Face Docker Spaces (now need PRO) and Cloudflare Containers (Workers Paid only) are not viable free options.

A 15-minute poll uses ~62 CU-h/month, a 30-minute poll ~31. Waking the DB on every Gmail push, or polling faster than every 5 minutes, keeps it awake almost permanently (~182 CU-h at 0.25 CU). That goes over 100 CU-h and SUSPENDS THE DATABASE until next month.
- Neon's 5 GB/month public network transfer is per project and shared by Postgres, Object Storage and Functions. Running out suspends the compute. Serving screenshots from the same project as the DB can therefore take the DB offline. Put screenshots in a separate Neon project (Free allows 100).
- Hitting Neon's 0.5 GB storage limit makes even DELETEs fail (Neon: 'inserts, updates, and deletes'), so you cannot simply delete your way back under. Alert at 60%/80% and run retention well before the limit. Whether the 6-hour restore history counts toward the 0.5 GB is unclear.
- Autoscaling up to 2 CU on Free burns CU-hours 8x faster than 0.25 CU. Pin the compute to 0.25 CU (min=max) unless measurements say otherwise.
- The Vercel-managed Neon integration injects owner-role credentials, and that role is a neon_superuser member with BYPASSRLS. Never connect the app with them; create app_rw with SQL (unchanged from v1.1).
- Vercel Blob Hobby's 2,000 advanced ops (put/copy/list, plus dashboard browsing) run out after ~600 emails with 3 screenshots each, and then Blob is LOCKED FOR 30 DAYS. Don't use it for per-email artifacts.
- Cloudflare R2's free tier is excellent, but enabling R2 requires a card or PayPal on file and it has no spend cap. The same applies to GCP: Pub/Sub, Cloud KMS, Secret Manager and even the Free Tier all need a billing account with a payment method. Google budgets only alert; they do not stop spending.
- Without a GCP billing account there is no Pub/Sub, so there is no Gmail push. Sync must be a polling loop (history.list, 2 quota units) driven by a free scheduler. Vercel Hobby cron runs only once a day (±59 min), so use a Neon scheduled Function Trigger or Upstash QStash.
- Google OAuth in 'Testing' status: Gmail refresh tokens die after 7 days. For a long-lived personal or friends setup, publish as 'In production' and stay unverified: warning screen, 100-user lifetime cap, no CASA.
- Resend Free can send to anyone other than your own account address only after you verify a domain you own. onboarding@resend.dev delivers only to the Resend account's own email.
- Anthropic spend-limit responses are not normal rate limits. Your own limit returns HTTP 400 invalid_request_error. The tier cap returns 429 with error_code enforced_spend_limit_reached and NO retry-after, and SDK auto-retries will just keep failing. Catch both and flip the app into 'AI paused' mode.
- Opus 5 runs adaptive thinking at effort 'high' by default, and thinking is billed as $25/MTok output. max_tokens caps thinking plus text combined, so a tight cap with thinking on can truncate the structured JSON and trigger a paid retry.
- A 5-minute prompt-cache write costs 1.25x input. It only pays off if another call reuses the prefix within 5 minutes (batches or backfills). For single on-demand clicks, caching usually doesn't help.
- The Claude 4.7+ tokenizer (used by Opus 5) produces about 30% more tokens than older models. Re-measure with count_tokens on claude-opus-5 and don't reuse older estimates.
- Prepaid Anthropic credits expire after 1 year and are non-refundable. Buy small amounts ($5-10) and keep auto-reload OFF, so an empty balance is a guaranteed hard stop.
- Sentry Developer allows only 1 user and 5k errors/month. Over quota, events are silently dropped (no outage). Keep tracing sampled low so a noisy error loop doesn't eat the month's quota.

**Gotchas**

- Testing mode is a trap for background sync. Any Gmail scope makes refresh tokens die 7 days after consent. Run the owner/friends project 'In production' (unverified) instead: no 7-day expiry, but the unverified screen and the 100-new-user lifetime cap apply.
- The 100-user cap covers the project's whole lifetime, cannot be reset, and Google says it 'might be adjusted' based on reputation and risk. Every distinct account that grants access counts, including the owner and any test or throwaway accounts. Never test with random accounts on the prod connector project. Use a separate dev project.
- In Testing mode, 'A test user consumes a project's test user quota once added to the project.' Removing a test user may not free the slot.
- Unverified consent screens show only the app's domain, not its name or logo. Friends will see 'Google hasn't verified this app' and must click Advanced > Go to <domain> (unsafe). Tell invitees in advance, or it looks like phishing.
- The PUBLISH APP button reportedly stays disabled until a home page URL, a privacy policy URL and an authorized domain are set, even for a single-user app (rclone docs). Ship a minimal home page and privacy page on the app domain before publishing.
- Pub/Sub, needed for Gmail push, requires a billing account (card). A Free Trial billing account auto-closes after 90 days or $300. Billing is then disabled, resources are stopped, and after 30 days they are deleted. On a 'no card' plan, drop push entirely and poll.
- Don't put token encryption on GCP KMS through a Free Trial billing account. When the trial auto-closes, KMS decrypts fail, every stored refresh token becomes undecryptable, and every user has to reconnect.
- If a Google Cloud project, and so its OAuth client, sees no token requests or config edits for 6 months, it is deleted automatically (after a 30-day email warning), and every token under it fails with deleted_client. Refresh tokens unused for 6 months also die. Keep at least one poll per day running and keep the project contact email monitored.
- A password change on a user's Google account revokes refresh tokens that carry Gmail scopes. Treat invalid_grant as 'reconnect needed', not as an error to retry.
- A 101st consent by the same account on the same client silently invalidates that account's oldest refresh token (100 per account per client). Don't use prompt=consent on every login. Use it only on first connect or reconnect.
- POST /revoke removes all scopes granted to the whole project. If login and connector share a project, a mailbox disconnect would also kill the login grant. Keep login (basic scopes) and connector (gmail.readonly) in separate projects, as v1.1 already does.
- Workspace-account friends can be blocked by their admin. gmail.readonly is on Google's 'high-risk' scope list, and setting Gmail to Restricted revokes untrusted apps' tokens (admin_policy_enforced). Advanced Protection accounts may block most non-Google apps.
- 'Internal' user type only helps if the owner's mailbox is on a Workspace domain whose organization owns the project. Everyone else gets org_internal. Workspace is paid and Cloud Identity Free has no Gmail, so this is not a free way to get rid of the warning.
- Users' Google Security Checkup may flag the app as 'risky and unverified'. Expect friends to ask.
- Vercel Hobby cron runs at most once a day, so it can't drive a 15-minute poll. You need another trigger, which the scheduler research covers. Gmail quota is not the bottleneck: polling 100 mailboxes every 10 minutes uses about 0.04% of the daily threshold.
- Polling one job per mailbox per tick uses up execution-metered free tiers quickly: Inngest Free pauses at 50k executions a month, which is passed at about 18 mailboxes on a 15-minute schedule. Use one batched tick that checks getProfile.historyId and fans out only for mailboxes that changed.
- history.list returns 404 if the cursor is older than Google's history window (usually at least a week, sometimes hours). Make the 404 path a bounded resync of recent days, not a full 90-day backfill that would blow the free-tier budgets.
- Being exempt from verification is NOT an exemption from policy. The User Data Policy, Limited Use, the in-app disclosure and affirmative consent, token encryption at rest, key management and prompt-injection protection all still apply in the policy text.
